Telredor follow-up: state changes for reused point-shadow cubes

The large tester log identifies point shadows as a remaining bounded workload:
mean GPU 0.618 ms, p95 2.114 ms and maximum 10.004 ms across sampled passes.
Those samples include dynamic draws and unrelated GPU stalls. They are not a
measurement of the static-only optimization below.

renderPointShadow already retained complete static-only cube faces, but still
issued 22 device state calls before discovering that every face could be reused:
13 sampler unbinds, 3 MRT unbinds, 4 stream-frequency resets and 2 render states.
Defer that setup until the first face requiring a draw. Keep face-content
generation bookkeeping, refresh scheduling, face cycling and failure handling.
No extra cache, quality reduction, light removal or shader change is needed.

Both control and candidate pass with the same corrected setup.

The separate authored GPU regression passes on native D3D9 and DXVK. It covers
moving lights, live/static caster transitions, partial-face refresh, skipped
capture, upload handoff, offscreen re-entry and an already-bound cube. A wholly
reused cube preserves caller bindings; a forced rebuild unbinds it and produces
the identical six faces. Selection and log-format checks also pass.

Paired timing

Three executions per variant, in control/candidate/candidate/control/control/
candidate order, with no concurrent compilation. Each execution retains the
eight ABBA visibility blocks, 64 updates per block and two priming updates.
Compare the visibility-enabled blocks only. Take each view's median across
blocks and runs, then the geometric mean of candidate/control ratios over the
eight views. Lower completion time by light limit: 4 = 0.88%, 16 = 1.14%,
64 = 1.00%, 128 = 2.11%. Completion includes CPU submission and GPU execution.

This is a small overhead reduction, with visible timing noise. At limit 64 the
median across views is 0.283357 -> 0.284502 ms; the per-view ratio aggregation
above is a different statistic. At limit 128 it is 0.327586 -> 0.316929 ms.
These numbers do not justify a large performance or whole-frame FPS claim.
